Total: 100 points, plus up to 5 bonus points (Section 6).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HOW TO USE THIS RUBRIC
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Unlike CS 1-4, every submission attacks a different open problem (CS 1's mu,
  CS 2's crash risk, CS 3's driftless-Brownian-motion assumption, or CS 4's
  single-sigma smile), on data the student may have pulled themselves. This
  rubric is deliberately topic-agnostic: every row below is a property any
  serious quantitative attack on ANY of the four named problems must have, not
  a checklist tied to one case study's specific formulas. Map the generic
  language onto the student's specific choice of method before scoring.
- Section point totals (1=15, 2=25, 3=30, 4=15, 5=15) match the written report
  and live presentation described in the prompt's Deliverables section.
- Part A (Presentation & Reproducibility) is scored as DEDUCTIONS from the
  earned section total, capped at -15 points overall. The hard caps in A1
  (non-running code) apply on top of that cap.
- Within each section, every row lists the criterion, its point value, and what
  full / partial / no credit looks like. Partial credit is assessed per row.
- An attempted-but-flawed answer always scores higher than a skipped one. A
  criterion left unaddressed earns 0; the same criterion attempted badly earns
  something.
- Grading draws on both the written report and the live presentation; a claim
  made in one but not supported in the other (a number quoted live that
  doesn't appear in the report, or vice versa) is graded on whichever source
  actually substantiates it.
- This is a project about a genuine difficulty. A negative or mixed result,
  honestly reported, is NOT penalized anywhere in this rubric relative to a
  positive one — see 3d and A8. Penalize spin, not disappointing results.

================================================================================
PART A -- PRESENTATION & REPRODUCIBILITY  (cross-cutting; deductions, max -15)
================================================================================

A1. CODE RUNS TOP-TO-BOTTOM WITHOUT ERROR
    - Clean run, no errors: no deduction.
    - Runs with harmless warnings only (deprecation notices, solver
      convergence chatter that does not change results): no deduction.
    - One or two cells/scripts raise errors but the rest is independent and
      gradeable: -5, and the failing part is graded on its visible saved
      output only.
    - Code errors early and large portions cannot execute: FINAL SCORE CAPPED
      AT 60% of otherwise-earned points.
    - No runnable code submitted at all (report only, with no way to verify
      any reported number): not accepted for full credit; see syllabus
      format/late policy.

A2. SELF-CONTAINED AND REPRODUCIBLE
    - Data is obtained programmatically (via a documented API/download call or
      a cached file shipped with the submission) and a grader with a clean
      environment can reproduce every reported number: no deduction.
    - Depends on a local file or manual step that cannot be regenerated or
      wasn't shipped with the submission: -5.
    - Requires undocumented manual steps: -3.
    - Random seeds are set (or results are shown to be stable without them)
      for every stochastic step (simulation, bootstrap, resampling, MCMC,
      random initialization): -2 if missing AND results are visibly
      seed-sensitive.

A3. SECTION HEADINGS AND NAVIGATION
    - The report has a clear structure that makes it obvious where the
      problem statement, baseline, method, and evaluation each live, in that
      logical order: no deduction.
    - Structure present but some parts are buried or not clearly delineated:
      -3.
    - No real structure; grader has to hunt for where each piece of the
      argument lives: -6.

A4. FIGURES
    - All plots render and are present in the final report: no deduction.
    - Titles, axis labels, and legends (wherever more than one series or
      variant is shown) are present on every figure: up to -5 total, scaled to
      how many plots are unlabeled.
    - Figures illegible (too small, overlapping, default styling that hides
      the point being made): -2.

A5. WRITTEN INTERPRETATION QUALITY
    - Prose actually answers what the student set out to show, cites specific
      numbers and figures, and is in the student's own words: no deduction.
    - Interpretation is vague, generic, or just restates the setup without
      engaging the results: up to -5 across the whole report.

A6. CONSISTENT CONVENTIONS AND A FIXED, APPLES-TO-APPLES EVALUATION WINDOW
    - Units, sign conventions, and cost/friction assumptions are each stated
      explicitly once and held consistent everywhere; the baseline and the
      student's new method are evaluated over the identical data window and
      cost assumptions (per Section 3b): no deduction.
    - A convention switched partway through, or the baseline and new method
      silently evaluated on different data/windows/assumptions: -3 here (plus
      any Section 3 correctness loss), and flagged per A8.

A7. CODE HYGIENE
    - Reasonably organized; repeated logic is factored into functions; no dead
      cells/scripts that error; the student's new method is built as an
      actual extension of (or a clean alternative to) the original case
      study's code rather than an unrelated one-off script: no deduction.
    - Egregiously disorganized, or no discernible connection to the case
      study's own framework at all: -2.

A8. RED FLAGS (checked in context of Sections 2-3, deducted there, but flagged
    explicitly in feedback):
    - Lookahead bias / data leakage: fitting anything (the new method's
      parameters, a regime label, a hedge ratio) on data the method could not
      have seen at that point in time.
    - Cherry-picking the flattering metric or the flattering time window, with
      an unfavorable result quietly omitted or buried.
    - Comparing the new method against the baseline on different data,
      different frictions, or a different evaluation period without
      disclosure.
    - Treating "it worked once, on one run/seed/window" as a general
      conclusion, with no robustness check at all (see 3c).
    - Submitting a cosmetic extension of the original case study (more
      tickers, a longer window, a different seed) dressed up as a new method
      -- this is the specific failure mode the prompt's "What Counts as
      Embellishment" section warns against, and should be caught in Section 1
      before it costs the student in Section 2-3.

MAXIMUM TOTAL PART A DEDUCTION: -15 (A1 hard caps apply on top of this).

SECTION 1 -- PROBLEM SELECTION AND FRAMING   (15 points)
================================================================================

1a. Names the specific open problem being attacked ....................... 4
    Full: report states, in the first page, which of the four named open
          problems (or a pre-approved alternative) is being attacked, and
          restates precisely what the difficulty is in the student's own
          words -- not a restatement of the assignment prompt's text.
    Partial: topic is named but the specific difficulty (as opposed to the
             case study's topic generally) is not clearly stated (2).

1b. Explicit, well-defined baseline ....................................... 4
    Full: the comparison point for the whole project is named up front and is
          the relevant case study's own original approach (sample-mean mu,
          the undefended carry basket, the zero-friction/driftless AS policy,
          or the single flat-sigma Black-Scholes hedge), reproduced rather
          than assumed.
    Partial: a baseline is named but is not actually the case study's original
             method, with no justification for the substitution (2).
    None: no baseline identified at all; "improvement" asserted with nothing
          to improve on (0).

1c. Hypothesis / mechanism for why the method should help ................ 4
    Full: report states, before showing results, a specific mechanical reason
          the chosen method should address the stated difficulty (e.g. "a
          regime overlay should reduce drawdown because it cuts exposure
          before/during the correlated-unwind episodes that make carry's skew
          negative" -- not just "this method is known to be better").
    Partial: a method is chosen with no stated mechanism connecting it to the
             specific difficulty (2).

1d. Appropriate scope (not a cosmetic extension) .......................... 3
    Full: the project is a focused attack on the stated difficulty, matching
          the prompt's "Acceptable" bar -- a genuinely different estimator,
          overlay, policy, or pricing model, not the original case study
          re-run with more data, more tickers, or a different seed.
    Partial: scope is borderline -- a real methodological change, but a small
             one relative to the difficulty of the stated problem (1.5).
    None: scope is a re-run of the case study's existing sections with more
          data/tickers/seeds and no new method: 0, and flagged per A8.

================================================================================
SECTION 2 -- DATA AND METHODOLOGY   (25 points)
================================================================================

2a. Data pipeline ......................................................... 5
    Full: data (reused from the case study, or newly pulled) is obtained and
          cached with the same rigor CS 1-4 required: programmatic
          acquisition, a documented caching/invalidation scheme, a portable
          path.
    Partial: data pipeline works but cache invalidation or portability is
             undocumented or broken (2.5).

2b. Correct implementation of the chosen method ........................... 10
    Full: the method is implemented in a way that matches its own stated
          mathematics/statistics (e.g., a Bayesian approach has specified and
          justified priors; a shrinkage estimator states its target and
          shrinkage intensity; a regime model states its states and
          transition assumptions; SABR/local-vol states its calibration
          objective and fitted parameters) -- not a plausible-looking black
          box with unstated internals.
    Partial: method runs and produces plausible output, but one or more key
             modeling choices are unstated or unjustified (5); method is
             implemented in a way that doesn't actually match its own
             standard formulation (3).

2c. No leakage; correct in-sample/out-of-sample discipline ................ 5
    Full: whatever the new method estimates, calibrates, or fits is fit only
          on information available at that point in (simulated or real) time;
          the same discipline is enforced for both the baseline and the new
          method.
    Partial: leakage present in a minor or clearly-caveated way (2.5).
    None: leakage drives the headline result and is not acknowledged (0), and
          flagged per A8.

2d. Assumptions and limitations stated .................................... 5
    Full: the report explicitly states what the new method assumes that the
          original case study's method did not (or vice versa), and what
          would have to be true for the method's advantage to hold up outside
          the tested window/data.
    Partial: assumptions are implicit in the code but never stated in prose
             (2.5).

================================================================================
SECTION 3 -- EVALUATION AGAINST THE BASELINE   (30 points)
================================================================================

3a. Baseline faithfully reproduced ......................................... 5
    Full: the Section 1b baseline is actually run (not quoted from memory or
          from the original case study's writeup) on the same data window
          used to evaluate the new method, and its results are reported in
          full.
    Partial: baseline numbers are reused from the original case study on a
             different data window instead of being re-run on the project's
             own window (2.5), and flagged per A6.

3b. Apples-to-apples comparison ............................................ 8
    Full: baseline and new method are compared on identical data, identical
          cost/friction assumptions, and identical evaluation metrics; any
          difference in setup between the two is explicitly justified rather
          than incidental.
    Partial: comparison is mostly fair but one dimension (costs, window, or
             metric set) differs without full justification (4), and flagged
             per A6/A8.
    None: baseline and method are compared on visibly different data or
          assumptions with no acknowledgment (0-2), and flagged per A8.

3c. Statistical rigor of the comparison .................................... 10
    Full: the verdict rests on more than a single point estimate from a
          single run -- uncertainty is quantified via at least one of:
          multiple seeds/replicates, a bootstrap or resampling scheme, out-of-
          sample testing across more than one sub-period, or an explicit
          hypothesis test; the comparison reports a measure of spread/
          significance, not just a headline number.
    Partial: some robustness check attempted but it's thin (e.g. one extra
             sub-period, no formal uncertainty measure) (5).
    None: the entire verdict rests on one run with one number and no
          uncertainty quantification at all (0-2), and flagged per A8.

3d. Honest verdict and tradeoff ............................................. 7
    Full: the report states plainly whether the method helped, by
          approximately how much (in the units relevant to the chosen
          problem -- Sharpe, drawdown, hedging P&L variance, pricing error),
          and what was given up to get it (lower average return, added
          complexity, calibration risk, fewer fills). A result that is
          negative or weaker than hoped is reported with the same rigor as a
          positive one and is NOT penalized for being negative.
    Partial: a verdict is given but the tradeoff/cost side is missing or
             hand-waved (3.5).
    None: result is reported as unambiguously positive with no discussion of
          cost or a counterfactual where it might not hold (0-1), and flagged
          per A8 if this looks like cherry-picking.

================================================================================
SECTION 4 -- INTERPRETATION AND STAKEHOLDER RECOMMENDATION   (15 points)
================================================================================

4a. Specific, evidence-referenced interpretation ........................... 8
    Full: at least one paragraph per major result that cites the actual
          numbers and figures produced (not restated from the method
          description), explaining what they mean for the stated open
          problem specifically.
    Partial: interpretation present but generic, or disconnected from the
             specific figures/numbers it should be citing (4).

4b. Stakeholder-facing recommendation ....................................... 7
    Full: 3-5 sentences addressed to the same stakeholder the chosen case
          study was written for (PM for CS 1/2, head trader for CS 3, desk
          head for CS 4), grounded in the Section 3 results, stating whether
          and under what conditions/caveats the new method should be adopted
          in place of the original case study's approach.
    Partial: present but generic, not decision-oriented, or not grounded in
             the specific numbers produced (3.5).

================================================================================
SECTION 5 -- LIVE PRESENTATION   (15 points)
================================================================================

5a. Clear problem/approach/result arc within the time limit ................ 6
    Full: the talk states the open problem being attacked, the baseline, the
          method, and the headline result clearly enough that an audience
          member who hasn't read the report can follow it, within the
          allotted time.
    Partial: covers the material but runs significantly over/under time or
             skips one of the four elements (3).

5b. Supporting visuals ....................................................... 4
    Full: slides/figures used in the talk are legible, labeled, and match
          what's in the written report (no numbers quoted live that
          contradict or aren't supported by the report).
    Partial: visuals present but hard to read live, or a live claim isn't
             backed by the report (2).

5c. Handles questions ......................................................... 5
    Full: can explain and defend specific methodological choices (why this
          baseline, why this robustness check, why this metric) when asked,
          rather than restating the slide.
    Partial: answers questions but mostly by restating prepared material
             rather than engaging with the specific question asked (2.5).

================================================================================
SECTION 6 -- BONUS   (up to +5 points, optional)
================================================================================

  +3  A genuinely additional robustness check beyond what Section 3c already
      required: a second, independent baseline; testing the method across
      more than one case study's worth of data/assets; or an explicit
      sensitivity analysis showing how the headline result changes as a key
      assumption (cost level, prior strength, calibration window) is varied.
  +2  Clear discussion of what would be needed to take the method from this
      project to something a real desk could actually deploy (monitoring,
      a kill-switch, retraining/recalibration cadence, or similar), grounded
      in the project's own findings rather than generic boilerplate.

  Partial bonus is allowed. There is no penalty for skipping this section.
